# Prompt v1 Acceptance Criteria v0

## SECTION 1 — ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA v0

| ID | Requirement | Pass condition | Fail condition | Source basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AC-01** | **Conform exactly to the agreed structured-output schema.** | Output is valid against the corrected JSON schema: all required top-level and nested fields are present, field types are valid, and no undeclared properties are added where `additionalProperties: false`. | Required fields are missing, field structure/types are invalid, or unsupported extra fields are introduced. | Corrected JSON schema defines the output contract explicitly. |
| **AC-02** | **Use only the allowed relevance and priority labels.** | `relevance` is exactly `Relevant`, `Uncertain`, or `Not relevant`; `priority` is exactly `High Priority`, `Medium Priority`, `Watchlist`, or `Informational`. | Any different, renamed, combined, numeric, or invented category is returned. | Enumerated values in the corrected schema; Amy also proposed the four priority categories for the briefing. |
| **AC-03** | **Keep source-grounded findings supported by the supplied publication evidence.** | Factual findings, statistics and source descriptions can be traced to the supplied article/source material or supplied quantitative inputs. | The output presents a factual claim, statistic, trend or event as source-grounded when it is not supported by the supplied evidence. | The proposal requires every claim/number to trace to real source evidence; the frozen reference answer applies this discipline finding by finding. |
| **AC-04** | **Do not convert national/public-source evidence into unsupported Medibank-specific facts.** | Medibank-specific implications are clearly framed as interpretation, investigation questions or information gaps unless Medibank-specific evidence is supplied. | National ABS/AIHW evidence is stated as proving Medibank member prevalence, claims, utilisation, cost, coverage or operational impact without supporting evidence. | Amy said public trends should help Medibank decide what to investigate internally; the frozen reference explicitly keeps Medibank prevalence, claims, costs and coverage unknown. |
| **AC-05** | **Separate source evidence from Medibank interpretation and judgement.** | `source_grounded_findings` contains evidence from the publication; relevance, criteria judgements, priority and Medibank implications remain in `medibank_assessment`. | Interpretation is presented as source fact, or source evidence and Medibank judgement are materially blended so their origin cannot be distinguished. | The corrected schema deliberately separates these sections; the frozen reference answer follows the same division. |
| **AC-06** | **Preserve material quantitative and temporal context accurately.** | Values, units, populations/denominators, geography, publication/update/retrieval dates where supplied, reference periods, comparison periods and relevant comparators/baselines are preserved accurately. Different date types or periods are not substituted for one another. | A material figure, date, period, population, denominator, unit or comparator is altered, conflated, detached from its context, or invented. | These elements are explicit fields in the schema, and the frozen reference treats such contextual fidelity as part of verification. |
| **AC-07** | **Distinguish observed values, estimates, projections and other qualified measures.** | Estimates, projections, modelled results, registered-year measures or similar qualified values retain their material status when the source makes that distinction. | A projection is described as an observed future change, an estimate as a known count, or another materially qualified measure as an unqualified fact. | The frozen reference repeatedly preserves distinctions such as estimated prevalence, projected growth and modelled burden, and warns against converting them into observed change. |
| **AC-08** | **Represent missing or unsupported evidence explicitly rather than guessing.** | Where the evidence does not support a field or claim, use `null`, `not stated`, or an equivalent schema-compliant representation, and record important gaps where appropriate. | Missing information is filled using assumption, invented detail or unsupported extrapolation. | The corrected schema explicitly instructs use of `null` / `not stated`; the frozen reference uses this throughout and records information gaps. |
| **AC-09** | **Preserve material source caveats and scope limitations.** | Caveats that materially affect interpretation—such as denominator scope, principal-diagnosis limits, preliminary data, estimation uncertainty or unavailable coverage—are retained in `source_caveat` or otherwise clearly represented. | A finding is materially overstated because a source qualification or scope limitation has been omitted or contradicted. | `source_caveat` is a required finding field; the frozen reference treats caveats as necessary to prevent misleading interpretation. |
| **AC-10** | **Use the agreed Medibank relevance/prioritisation factors rather than substituting unrelated criteria.** | Relevance and priority judgements are based on the supplied Medibank criteria, with clinical significance, utilisation/demand, population affected, magnitude of change and cost implications as the core factors, supplemented where supported by trend continuity, private-health relevance, emerging treatment/model of care, evidence strength and actionability. | The judgement ignores the agreed criteria or replaces them with an unrelated prioritisation framework. | Amy directly ranked and explained the core factors and additional considerations; the proposal adopts those factors as the basis of the relevance/prioritisation stage. |
| **AC-11** | **Do not invent unavailable quantitative calculations; use supplied/pre-computed metrics faithfully.** | When quantitative metrics such as magnitude, population or trend duration are supplied or pre-computed, they are used as given and interpreted appropriately; unavailable quantitative values are left unsupported rather than fabricated. | The LLM invents a percentage, rate, magnitude, denominator or other calculation that was neither supplied nor validly supported by the input evidence. | The proposal specifies a hybrid design in which measurable factors are calculated by code and supplied to the LLM, while the frozen reference avoids adopting unsupported derived calculations. |
| **AC-12** | **Maintain source traceability without inventing source locations.** | Each material finding is traceable to the supplied evidence using `supporting_evidence` and, where available, an accurate `source_location`. If a precise source location is unavailable, it is represented as `null` / `not stated` rather than invented. | A material finding cannot be traced to supplied evidence, or a source location/evidence path is fabricated. | Traceability is an explicit project requirement and evaluation check; the schema requires both `supporting_evidence` and `source_location`, while also allowing missing location details to be represented explicitly rather than fabricated. |

## SECTION 2 — DEFERRED TO THE FULL EVALUATION RUBRIC

The following dimensions should be evaluated after Prompt v1 is tested rather than frozen now as binary acceptance conditions:

- **Completeness of findings:** whether the prompt identifies enough of the important evidence without requiring exhaustive transcription. The frozen reference explicitly states that its selected findings are representative rather than exhaustive.
- **Quality of relevance reasoning:** how well the model integrates the Medibank criteria rather than merely mentioning them.
- **Quality of priority reasoning:** whether the chosen category is well calibrated to clinical significance, utilisation, scale, change, cost, private-health relevance and actionability.
- **Borderline-category judgement:** especially distinctions such as High vs Medium or Watchlist vs Informational, where Amy deliberately avoided universal numerical thresholds.
- **Consistency across repeated runs:** the proposal explicitly identifies rating stability as something to test empirically rather than assume in advance.
- **Appropriate level of detail and concision:** whether outputs contain enough evidence and explanation without becoming unnecessarily long.
- **Relative importance and selection of multiple valid findings:** including whether the model focuses attention on the developments most useful to Medibank.
- **Quality of follow-up / monitoring suggestions:** beyond the hard requirement that they must not claim unsupported Medibank facts.
- **Handling of genuinely conflicting evidence across sources:** important for later cross-source work, but not suitable as a universal pre-check for every individual ABS/AIHW article. Amy's instruction is to surface credible conflicts rather than silently choose one.
- **Prompt efficiency and cost:** useful for choosing between candidate prompts, but not a substantive correctness requirement of an individual output.
- **Degree of improvement over an unstructured LLM summary:** this is part of the project's final evaluation design, not a binary requirement to freeze before Prompt v1 testing.

## SECTION 3 — CONFLICT / AMBIGUITY CHECK

There is **no genuine conflict in the authoritative materials that prevents the criteria above from being used as Acceptance Criteria v0**.

There are, however, two areas intentionally **not** converted into harder rules:

1. **Prioritisation thresholds:** Amy gave a clear ordering and rationale for the prioritisation factors but explicitly indicated that their importance is context-dependent and did **not** define fixed numerical thresholds. Therefore Acceptance Criteria v0 should require use of the agreed factors, not prescribe a scoring formula or threshold.

2. **Exact set/number of findings:** the frozen reference answer is deliberately representative rather than exhaustive, so it does not justify a hard rule requiring a model to reproduce a fixed number or exact set of findings.
